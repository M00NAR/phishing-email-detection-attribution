                                                Abstract
          Phishing attacks are evolving rapidly with the use of AI tools that can write convincing,
      error-free emails. Most traditional filters look for human errors or simple keyword triggers,
      which AI emails easily avoid. In this paper, we propose a direct, statistics-driven framework
      to detect phishing emails and determine whether they were written by a human or generated
      by an AI. Instead of feeding raw text directly into complex models, we extract 14 distinct
      linguistic, stylistic, and structural features from each email.
          We run every feature through a strict statistical pipeline: distribution checks (Shapiro-
      Wilk and Levene’s tests), multi-group difference tests (One-Way ANOVA and Kruskal-Wallis),
      and categorical frequency tests (Chi-Square). Features are only kept if their differences are
      statistically significant under False Discovery Rate adjustments and meet clear effect-size
      thresholds. We then train three separate Naive Bayes sub-models on these verified feature
      groups. Finally, a Meta-Naive Bayes model combines the output probabilities into a two-step
      decision: first, whether the email is legitimate or phishing; second, if phishing, whether
      it is human-written or AI-generated. On a balanced dataset of 3,000 emails, our pipeline
      yields accurate, transparent, and fully explainable probability scores for every classification
      decision.

   Keywords: Phishing Detection, AI-Generated Text, Hypothesis Testing, Naive Bayes,
Feature Selection, Statistical Analysis.


1     Problem Statement
Phishing emails remain the most common entry point for cyber attacks. Historically, security
systems and human users could spot phishing messages by looking for simple clues:
    1. Spelling errors, broken grammar, and awkward phrasing.

    2. High-pressure urgency keywords (e.g., “account suspended immediately”).

    3. Irregular sentence lengths and disorganized email structures.
   With modern AI text generators, these clues are no longer reliable. AI-generated phishing
emails are grammatically clean, follow standard professional formatting, and use more subtle
language. This creates two distinct challenges:
    • Detection: Differentiating between normal, legitimate emails and phishing attempts that
      contain no obvious errors.

    • Attribution: Determining whether a detected phishing email was written by an individual
      human attacker or generated by an automated AI system.

   Our goal is to build an interpretable system that uses clear statistical rules to solve both
challenges, providing exact mathematical reasons for every classification decision.

2.1    Linguistic Features Extracted
We extract 14 quantifiable metrics from each email across four core areas:

    • Text Complexity and Predictability: Average token log-probability and sentence
      perplexity variance (burstiness). AI text is typically very uniform across sentences, while
      human writing varies naturally.

    • Structure and Syntax: Standard deviation of sentence length, maximum parse-tree
      depth, and paragraph density.


                                                2
    • Language Mechanics: Orthographic error density (spelling and typos) and punctuation
      distribution entropy.

    • Communication Style: Density of urgency keywords, pronoun balance (ratio of first-
      person to second-person words), and sentiment variance across sentences.

5     Summary of System Characteristics
    1. Direct Statistical Gating: Features are included only after clearing formal hypothesis
       testing, keeping the input space small and theoretically sound.

    2. Full Auditability: Because the entire pipeline relies on standard probability distributions
       and Bayes’ theorem, every classification can be traced back to exact feature likelihoods
       and probability scores.

    3. Low Compute Overhead: The system relies entirely on straightforward mathemati-
       cal formulas and Naive Bayes inference, enabling sub-10ms classification times without
       specialized hardware.
